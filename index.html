Beyond the workout

Sports and games make movement meaningful.

Explore recreational activities, learn the basics and discover how enjoyable participation can support an active lifestyle. Choose inclusive, age-appropriate activities and adapt them to your ability.

⚽

Team sports

Football, basketball, volleyball and other team games can build coordination, communication, cooperation and a sense of belonging.

🏸

Individual & racket sports

Badminton, tennis, table tennis, cycling and swimming offer ways to practise skills, balance, stamina and personal progress.

🎲

Traditional games & recreation

Traditional games, dance and playful outdoor activities can make movement social, accessible and enjoyable across generations.

Participate safely: Warm up, use suitable equipment and protective gear, drink water, respect the rules and take breaks. Increase activity gradually. Stop and seek appropriate help for significant pain, fainting, chest discomfort or unusual breathing difficulty.
Everyday care & health education

Understand supportive home care—without replacing proper treatment.

Simple self-care may ease minor, short-lived discomfort, but a home remedy is not a guaranteed cure. The right response depends on the symptom, age and health circumstances.

💧

Hydration & rest

For many minor illnesses, drinking enough fluids and allowing time to rest can support general recovery. Follow medical advice when fluid intake is restricted.

🍵

Comfort measures

A warm drink may soothe a dry or irritated throat. Avoid ingredients that trigger allergies, and never give honey to a child under 12 months.

🧼

Everyday prevention

Handwashing, safe food handling, clean surroundings and appropriate wound care help reduce avoidable health risks.

Health safety: Vital Noval shares general education, not diagnosis or treatment instructions. Do not use home remedies to delay care, stop prescribed medication or treat serious symptoms. Seek urgent medical help for emergencies, severe or worsening symptoms, breathing difficulty, chest pain, confusion or signs of dehydration. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for persistent symptoms, children, pregnancy or existing health conditions.

First aid & emergency readiness

Know what to do while help is on the way.

First-aid awareness can help people recognise an emergency, act within their training and seek professional help quickly. These topic summaries are educational signposts, not a replacement for hands-on, certified training.

🩹

Severe bleeding

Recognise life-threatening bleeding, call local emergency services and apply firm, continuous direct pressure with a clean dressing or cloth. For life-threatening limb bleeding, a commercial tourniquet may be appropriate when available and the responder knows how to use it.

🔥

Burns & scalds

Learn immediate first-aid priorities, including cooling a burn with cool running water, protecting the area and recognising burns that need urgent medical assessment. Avoid applying butter, oils or home remedies.

🫁

Choking response

Understand how to recognise severe choking and why the response differs by age and circumstance. Learn the correct techniques through an accredited, hands-on first-aid course.

❤️

CPR & AED awareness

Learn to recognise possible cardiac arrest, activate emergency services, locate an AED and follow its voice prompts. Practical CPR skills should be learned and refreshed through recognised training.

🦴

Injuries & fractures

Explore how to protect an injured person, avoid unnecessary movement, recognise serious injury and arrange appropriate emergency care.

📞

Emergency action plan

Know your local emergency number, share the exact location, describe what happened, follow the call handler's instructions and make the scene as safe as possible.

Emergency reminder: If someone has life-threatening bleeding, trouble breathing, is unresponsive or appears seriously injured, contact local emergency services immediately. Do not delay emergency care to read website material. For bleeding, maintain firm direct pressure while help is coming; use a tourniquet only when indicated and you are trained or can follow reliable device instructions.
