Everyday wellness

1. A practical healthy-living starter

Build a realistic foundation with small habits that can fit into ordinary days.

Open learning guide
  • Choose one change: pick a habit that feels manageable rather than changing everything at once.
  • Make movement part of the day: take comfortable opportunities to walk, stretch or enjoy an activity suited to your ability.
  • Support recovery: make room for rest and a regular sleep routine where possible.
  • Eat with variety: include a range of nourishing foods and drink fluids regularly, taking account of any professional advice you have received.
  • Review gently: notice what helped, what got in the way and what small adjustment you can try next.

Try it: Write down one healthy habit you will practise this week and when you plan to do it.

Movement & fitness

2. Moving more: a beginner's guide

Explore safe ways to begin or gradually return to physical activity.

Open learning guide
  • Start at a comfortable level and choose activities you enjoy, such as walking, gentle cycling, dancing or chair-based movement.
  • Begin with manageable sessions; increase duration or effort gradually rather than making sudden jumps.
  • Include a gentle warm-up and cool-down, and allow time for recovery.
  • Use supportive footwear and suitable surroundings. Adapt movements to your balance, mobility and ability.
  • Stop if you experience chest discomfort, faintness, unusual shortness of breath or significant pain; seek appropriate medical advice.

Try it: Plan a few short movement breaks this week that fit your schedule and ability.

Nutrition

3. Understanding balanced meals

Learn a flexible way to think about food variety and everyday nourishment.

Open learning guide
  • Aim for variety across vegetables and fruits, protein foods, grains or other starchy staples, and suitable sources of fats.
  • Choose whole or minimally processed options when practical, while recognising that convenience foods can also have a place.
  • Drink water regularly; needs vary with climate, activity, age and health.
  • Respect cultural food traditions, budget, access, allergies and personal preferences.
  • There is no single perfect meal plan for everyone. Seek individual advice from a qualified dietitian or healthcare professional when managing a medical condition or special dietary need.

Try it: Add one food you enjoy from a different food group to a meal this week.

Sports & recreation

4. Enjoying sports and games safely

Make participation welcoming, enjoyable and appropriate for different abilities.

Open learning guide
  • Choose an activity that matches participants' age, experience, interests and abilities.
  • Explain rules, respect others and encourage inclusive participation over pressure to win.
  • Warm up, use suitable equipment and protective gear, and take water and rest breaks.
  • Increase training gradually and allow recovery between demanding sessions.
  • Stop activity and seek appropriate help if someone has a serious injury, chest discomfort, fainting or difficulty breathing.

Try it: Choose one enjoyable group or individual activity and identify one way to make it more accessible.

Safety & first aid

5. Emergency readiness: what to learn

Prepare to recognise an emergency, contact help and act within your training.

Open learning guide
  • Know the local emergency number and the exact address or location where you are.
  • Learn first aid, CPR and AED use through a recognised hands-on course; techniques can differ by age and situation.
  • For severe bleeding, emergency services should be contacted immediately. Firm, continuous direct pressure is a key first-aid measure while help is coming; tourniquets require appropriate use and training or reliable device instructions.
  • Keep a stocked first-aid kit accessible and check its contents regularly.
  • Make an emergency plan for your home, workplace or group, including who calls for help and how responders can find you.
Important: This is an awareness checklist, not emergency treatment instruction. In a life-threatening situation, contact local emergency services immediately and follow the call handler's directions. Do not delay care to read a guide.
Group learning

6. Planning a healthy-living session

A simple framework for educators, workplaces and community organisers.

Open learning guide
  • Set one learning goal: decide what participants should understand or practise by the end.
  • Know your audience: adapt language, activities and accessibility for age, culture, ability and local context.
  • Keep it practical: use a short discussion, a safe activity and time for questions.
  • Use trustworthy information: check health content against current public-health guidance and local policies.
  • Evaluate and improve: ask what participants learned and what they would like to explore next.
Institutional note: These resources are general educational starting points, not accredited curricula, clinical advice or certified first-aid training. Have relevant professionals review materials before formal use.
